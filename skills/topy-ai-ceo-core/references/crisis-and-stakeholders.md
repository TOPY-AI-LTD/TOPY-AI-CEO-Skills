# Crisis and Stakeholders

Use this reference for incidents, executive departures, board friction, restructuring, partner disputes, or any case where communication sequence matters.

## Crisis response levels

| Level | What it means | Response style |
|---|---|---|
| Level 1 | Annoying but contained | Assign owner, monitor, no broad escalation |
| Level 2 | Operational disruption | Stabilize, communicate to impacted team, set a deadline |
| Level 3 | Material business impact | Leadership involvement, clear containment plan, external messaging review |
| Level 4 | Existential or legal exposure | Immediate executive response, legal/HR/comms coordination |

## Crisis response sequence

1. Observe: what happened, who is affected, what is known, what is not known
2. Orient: what the blast radius is and whether the situation is worsening
3. Decide: minimum viable response in the next 2 hours
4. Act: assign owners and a check-in time
5. Reassess: decide whether the plan needs to tighten, broaden, or stop

## Immediate triage questions

- What is the user/customer impact?
- What systems, credentials, or records are exposed?
- What legal or contractual obligations exist?
- Who must hear this from us first?
- What is the likely rumor if we do nothing?

## Stakeholder mapping

Map each stakeholder by:

- power
- interest
- support level
- likely fear
- likely action if surprised

Common groups:

- board
- investors
- leadership
- affected team members
- customers
- partners
- regulators

## Communication sequence

Start private and narrow, then widen:

1. your strongest internal ally
2. the most powerful blocker or approver
3. the swing vote
4. the people directly affected
5. the broader organization
6. external partners or customers if needed

## Message framing

- board and investors: risk, control, and expected impact
- leadership: plan, ownership, and what changes now
- employees: stability, clarity, and what they should do next
- customers: service continuity and honest timing
- partners: continuity and specific next steps

## Resistance handling

If a stakeholder resists, diagnose the reason before answering:

- they do not understand the plan
- they disagree with the analysis
- they fear personal impact
- they are defending a political position
- they believe the decision violates company values

## Anti-patterns

- waiting for perfect certainty
- letting the wrong person announce the change first
- mixing containment with root cause
- sending one generic message to all audiences
- pretending a crisis is merely a communication problem

## Output checklist

1. Who needs to know now?
2. Who needs to know next?
3. What is the message for each audience?
4. What is the rollback or containment plan?
5. What is the review point if the first response does not hold?
